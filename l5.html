Lesson 5 – Customer Value and Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

Learning Outcomes

After completing this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Define and explain the concept of Customer Value.
  • Understand the meaning and importance of Customer Lifetime Value (CLV).
  • Identify factors that affect how customers perceive value.
  • Distinguish between high-value and low-value customers.
  • Apply strategies that increase CLV and improve customer retention.

Introduction

Not all customers provide the same level of benefit to a business. Some purchase frequently, refer friends, and remain loyal — these are high-value customers. Others buy occasionally and quickly switch brands. Understanding how valuable each customer is helps businesses prioritize efforts and maximize long-term profit.

What is Customer Value?

Customer Value measures how much benefit or satisfaction a customer gets from a product or service compared to the cost they pay. It answers: “Is this product worth it?”

When customers feel the benefits exceed the price, they’re more likely to buy again and recommend the product to others.

🧠 Quick Break Question 1

Which of the following best describes Customer Value?

  • The total revenue a business earns from all customers.
  • The satisfaction a customer receives compared to the price paid.
  • The number of customers a business serves each year.
  • The total number of repeat purchases a customer makes.

What is Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)?

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) is the total amount of money a business expects to earn from a customer during their entire relationship. It helps identify which customers contribute the most to long-term success.

How Businesses Increase CLV

  • Offer Loyalty Programs: Reward customers for repeat purchases.
  • Send Personalized Offers: Tailor promos based on purchase history.
  • Provide Excellent Customer Service: Respond quickly and effectively.
  • Follow-Up Marketing: Remind customers about new products or deals.
  • Encourage Referrals: Give discounts for bringing new customers.

🧠 Quick Break Question 2

Which of the following customers is likely to have a high Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)?

  • A customer who buys frequently and remains loyal for years.
  • A first-time buyer who never returns.
  • A customer who only buys during big discounts.
  • A person who visits but never makes a purchase.

Summary

Customer Value reflects the satisfaction a customer feels compared to what they pay, while Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) measures the total contribution of a customer over time. Businesses that focus on increasing CLV through excellent service and loyalty programs can achieve sustainable growth and success.
